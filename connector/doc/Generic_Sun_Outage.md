# Generic Sun Outage

## About

The **Generic Sun Outage** connector predicts sun outages (sun transit) for satellite earth stations. Twice a year, around the equinoxes, the sun passes behind a geostationary satellite as seen from an earth station. The noise of the sun then overwhelms the satellite signal, and the link can degrade or drop for a few minutes a day over several days.

For every earth station, the connector calculates when the sun will be inside the outage angle of the dish. It shows whether an outage is active right now and lists the upcoming outage windows with their start, peak, end, and duration. Earth stations and satellites can be entered manually or imported from provisioning files, and steerable antennas follow the live orientation reported by their controller element.

This is a virtual connector: all calculations run inside DataMiner, and no device connection is needed.

## Key Features

- **Current outage status**: Every earth station shows whether a sun outage is active now, the days until the next outage, and the next start and end time in UTC.

- **Future outage predictions**: The connector lists the upcoming outage windows for one to five equinox cycles, including the peak time, the duration, and the season.

- **Fixed and steerable antennas**: Fixed antennas are calculated from the earth station and satellite positions. Steerable antennas use the azimuth, elevation, and satellite reported by a controller element in the same DataMiner System.

- **Bulk provisioning from CSV files**: Earth stations and satellites can be imported automatically from semicolon-separated files, with Manual, Auto Sync, or Auto Delete reflection between the file and the tables.

- **Safe mix of manual and imported data**: Each earth station records whether it was imported or added manually. Manual stations are never overwritten or deleted by an import, and name conflicts are flagged and resolved automatically.

- **Outage overview**: A tree view groups each selected earth station with its predicted outages for quick review by operators.

## Use Cases

### Warning Operators Before a Sun Outage

**Challenge**: Sun outages are predictable, but without a per-site forecast, operators only notice them when links start to fail and customers call in.

**Solution**: The connector calculates the outage windows of every earth station and shows the days until the next outage, the start and end time, and whether an outage is active now.

**Benefit**: Operators can plan around the outage windows, inform customers in advance, and avoid unnecessary troubleshooting.

### Suppressing False Alarms During a Sun Outage

**Challenge**: During a sun outage, link and modem alarms are expected. They flood the Alarm Console and can create unnecessary trouble tickets.

**Solution**: Alarm monitoring, correlation rules, or ticketing workflows can check the status of an earth station and its outage windows to recognize alarms caused by sun transit.

**Benefit**: Fewer false alarms and tickets, and faster attention for the real issues.

### Keeping Large Earth Station Inventories Up to Date

**Challenge**: Networks with hundreds or thousands of remote terminals change every day, and maintaining the earth station list by hand is error-prone.

**Solution**: The connector periodically imports earth stations and satellites from provisioning files generated by an inventory system or another DataMiner connector. Stations that operators add manually are kept separately and are protected from the import.

**Benefit**: Predictions always cover the current network, with minimal manual work.

## Technical Reference

### Prerequisites

- **DataMiner 10.4.0** or higher is required for range 1.3.0.x of the connector.

- **Satellite longitudes** are needed for fixed earth stations. They can be added manually or imported from a satellites file.

- **A controller element** in the same DataMiner System is needed for each steerable antenna. The controller's protocol must expose the azimuth, elevation, and satellite as parameters or table columns.
